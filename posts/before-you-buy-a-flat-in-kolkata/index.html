Before You Buy a Flat in Kolkata: Read This True Story

A cautionary Kolkata property story explaining why buyers should independently verify ownership, title documents, mortgages, loans, and the seller before purchasing a flat.

A family in Kolkata saved for years and finally purchased what they believed was their secure home. A bank approved a housing loan, the available documents appeared proper, and registration was completed. They reasonably believed these steps meant the property was safe.

Later, they discovered that the flat had already been mortgaged in connection with an earlier loan. Recovery and auction proceedings followed, placing the family’s home and finances at serious risk.

This anonymised cautionary account illustrates an important lesson: a loan sanction, document appearance, or completed registration should not be treated as a substitute for independent and transaction-specific verification.

Why Buyers Can Develop a False Sense of Security

  • A bank has approved a home loan
  • The seller has produced photocopies or apparently complete papers
  • An agreement has been signed
  • The deed has been registered
  • The buyer has received possession

Each step may be important, but none should automatically be interpreted as a complete guarantee of ownership, title, mortgage status, authority, or freedom from disputes.

Verification Areas to Consider Before Paying

Ownership and Authority

Confirm who claims ownership, how that person obtained the property, and whether the person signing has lawful authority.

Title and Document History

Arrange an independent review of the chain of documents, descriptions, parties, schedules, and relevant records.

Mortgage and Loan Enquiries

Ask whether the property or original documents have been offered as security and identify checks appropriate to the transaction.

Original Documents

Understand where the originals are held, who can produce them, and whether an explanation for their absence is independently supportable.

Seller Background

Verify identity, address, authority, material representations, and any warning signs that require deeper enquiry.

Property and Authority Records

Consider taxes, approvals, possession, measurements, occupancy, litigation, notices, and other records relevant to the property type.

A Safer Decision Process

  1. Pause before paying: Do not allow urgency to replace verification.
  2. Collect the full information set: Obtain clear copies and inspect originals where appropriate.
  3. Use independent professionals: Engage qualified legal, technical, valuation, tax, or financial professionals according to the transaction.
  4. Record representations: Important claims, conditions, receipts, timelines, and responsibilities should be documented.
  5. Resolve inconsistencies: Missing originals, unexplained loans, name variations, mismatched descriptions, or unusual urgency require answers before commitment.

The Central Lesson

Buying a home may be the largest financial decision a family makes. Verification should therefore be independent, layered, and completed before substantial payment—not treated as an assumption arising from a loan or registration.
